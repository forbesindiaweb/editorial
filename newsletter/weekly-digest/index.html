Hello and welcome back to Forbes India Weekly Digest!

The weekend ushered in an end to the month-long protests seeking accountability for multiple examination paper leaks which has led to stress and confusion among the student community in India.

On Saturday, education minister Dharmendra Pradhan handed in his resignation. The mandate for Ministry of Education was handed over to Pralhad Joshi, who also holds the portfolio of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, and New and Renewable Energy.

The change in guard brings up the larger question – does India’s competitive examination structure and admission criteria to professional courses need to be revisited as a whole?

The government seems to have arrived at a similar conclusion, appointing Infosys Chairman Nandan Nilekani to lead a high-powered task force to overhaul the National Testing Agency (NTA), and making examinations leak-proof, transparent, and technology-driven. The NTA is an autonomous body formed by the government of India for conducting entrance examinations for admissions and fellowships for higher education.

Interestingly, a similar exercise was undertaken in 2024 after allegations of paper leak for NEET-UG (National Eligibility cum Entrance Test) examinations surfaced. A High-Powered Steering Committee chaired by Dr K Radhakrishnan, former chairman of ISRO, was appointed to look into reforms for NTA. The committee submitted its recommendations in October 20204 and according to its response to Supreme Court in May 2026, most of its recommendations have already been implemented.

The recommendations of the committee included a host of measures including sharper focus of the agency to restrict itself to conducting entrance examinations and not public recruitment exams, implementing biometric verification of candidates to prevent impersonation, a tiered federal-state coordination model for security, multi-stage testing, hybrid model of examination with encrypted papers printed at the examination centres, and more.

While the Nilekani-led task force’s recommendations are awaited, students preparing for entrance examinations will have to rely on the old ways.

📣FM Nirmala Sitharaman pushes for responsive tax governance

At the Income Tax Day celebrations on Friday, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman urged the IT department to adopt citizen-friendly measures, pushing for transparent administrative practices.

➤Sitharaman asked the department to prioritise a 5R framework – recognise, respond, redress, reflect, and reform.

➤Officers are required to acknowledge complaints promptly and communicate with clarity and empathy to resolve genuine grievances within set time limits.

➤The FM said that the department should focus on ‘grievance intelligence’ to analyse patterns of recurring issues across regions.

📖NEET protests highlight the need for admission reforms

Every year only 6 percent of the 22 lakh candidates appearing for NEET examinations stand a chance to secure an MBBS seat across the country. In this scenario, the recent paper leaks affect the students’ faith in a merit-based process and puts too much pressure on them to do well in a single examination on a set day. The entrance exam process in India needs a systemic change, believe educators.

➤Educators recommend a computer-based test, across multiple testing windows instead of a single annual sitting.

➤Shifting assessment from rote learnings to analytical skills and problem-solving abilities will lead to better outcomes.

➤The emphasis on a single test has led to the rise of dummy schools and coaching hubs cashing in on test prep rather than conceptual learning.
