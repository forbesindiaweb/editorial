The boardroom drama at India’s corporates was the talk of town this week as the tussle between Tata Sons and its largest shareholder, Tata Trusts played out in a board meeting held on Thursday.

After Tata Sons chairman N Chandrasekaran tendered his resignation on August 12, saying that he will not seek a reappointment for a third term as chairman after his tenure ends in February 2027 due to the delay by the board in coming to a conclusion on it, he is back at being the board’s pick for the job.

The board decided to approve fresh five-year term for Chandrasekaran, which is being contested by chairman of Tata Trusts, Noel Tata, who voted against the resolution. Tata Trusts, which owns 66 percent in Tata Sons, has said that the decision was a “legal nullity” since the appointment of a chairman for Tata Sons requires majority of the Trusts’ directors to vote in favour of the decision.

And then there is the push-and-pull battle on whether the conglomerate should be listed in the public markets. As of March 2024, Tata Sons had surrendered its certificate of registration so that it could be treated as an unregistered core investment company, to remove the regulatory requirement to list. However, on September 11 the Reserve Bank of India rejected the application, which makes the regulatory framework of upper-layer NBFC applicable to Tata Sons.

Tata Trusts has said that the conglomerate which is the holding company for multiple businesses, should consider all available options, including listing on the public markets.

This looks like only the beginning of a long process which will take a while to resolve and will be watched closely.

🔧Project Zorawar ends tank manufacturing monopoly

Project Zorawar, the indigenous light battle tank programme launched by DRDO in partnership with Larsen & Toubro (L&T) ends the country’s state-owned tank manufacturing monopoly. Driven by the need for lighter tanks at high altitudes during the Ladakh standoff, the move promises to open new defense opportunities.

➤Project Zorawar moved from concept to prototype in two years – a short turnaround time, when it comes to defense industry.

➤The compression in turnaround came from coordinating with the user, developer, and industry in parallel from day one, by reusing existing capabilities and iteration through trials.

➤The move has also seen Tata Advanced Systems and Mahindra Defence being cleared to build their own prototypes for armoured-vehicles, while state-owned facilities will work on anchoring legacy systems and mid-life upgrades for existing vehicles.

🛵TVS Motor to ramp up EV capacity

India’s largest two-wheeler maker, the TVS Motor Company is looking to ramp up its electric vehicle manufacturing capacity by 25 percent as demand continues to outpace supply. EV penetration in the two-wheeler market rose to 10.7 percent in August, climbing steeply since the West Asia conflict broke out.

➤The company which has a monthly manufacturing capacity of 40,000-45,000 units will raise it to 50,000-plus units over the quarter.

➤TVS remained cautiously optimistic about festive demand, expecting high single-digit growth during the period, due to macroeconomic conditions.

➤The company will enhance its premium motorcycle portfolio ahead of the festive season as the premium and super-premium segments contribute nearly 27 percent to industry volumes.

👟Nike’s bad run

Falling stocks and end of longstanding brand deals have kept sportswear company Nike in the news for all the wrong reasons. The company’s shares hit a 52-week low, even as Real Madrid football star Kylian Mbappe turned down renewing his decades long brand deal with Nike to join Swiss athletic wear brand On.

➤The brand will be removed from S&P 100 – the stock market index which tracks blue-chip companies from the US – on September 21, after 18 years.

➤Nike’s troubles are said to be rooted in management decisions as the company reduced the influence of specialised product teams, scaled back relationships with retail partners, in favour of moving marketing spends towards digital performance campaigns.

➤In India, the brand is endorsed by cricketer Shubman Gill, chosen as the face of Nike’s global Nike Mind campaign; cricketer Smriti Mandhana signing a multi-year partnership with the brand, and cricketer Shreyas Iyer becoming the only Indian athlete featured in Nike’s global “Why Do It?” campaign.
