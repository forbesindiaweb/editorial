Hello and welcome back to Forbes India Weekly Digest!

The Nifty 50 gained 0.7 percent on Friday, supported by buying in the Indian IT companies, following global sell-off in AI-linked stocks. Nifty IT gained 3.9 percent led by Tech Mahindra, after it reported better than expected Q2 results. In addition to Tech Mahindra, TCS, HCL Tech, and Infosys stocks outperformed Nifty 50.

The gain was hard-earned after a few tough months for Indian software companies which have been losing out due to concerns around AI eating into traditional business.

The current turn of events stems from global investor fears, questioning rising debt in AI infrastructure investments by hyperscalers, and whether it will result in actual gains in the long-term.

These concerns led to a sell-off in chipmakers in South Korea’s benchmark stock index KOSPI, closing at a six-month low on Friday, October 16. The Nasdaq composite fell 5 percent during the week – a record low since June, around concerns on high interest rates in the US and rising AI debt of the hyperscalers.

The rally in Indian IT stocks also has to do with value correction and their focus on growing AI-led revenues. As a wise man said, what goes up, comes down.

🚀India’s space tech companies reach escape velocity

India’s space-tech companies are eyeing the global markets for the next phase of growth especially in hardware and engineering services, with nearly 80 to 90 percent of their orders coming from customers abroad. Firms selling launch-vehicles and launch-linked services tend to have a global skew, while those dependent on Indian government and defense orders tend to the domestic markets. This has also resulted in an in-flow of foreign investors in the sector, as witnessed in recent funding rounds.

💊Global pharma rewrites India’s potential as R&D hub

Global pharma giants Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk are re-considering India’s position from a generic-drugs market which threatens their toplines, to that of a growing weight-loss drug market, and hub for drug development and potentially, manufacturing. Ensuring supply-chain diversification for starting materials amidst global unrest has had a significant role to play in this, along with India’s strong capabilities in AI-led R&D.

🚘Indian automakers bring the platform and engineering to global JVs

While China has managed to leverage its advanced knowledge in developing Electric Vehicles when it comes to joint ventures with global players, India is leveraging its expertise in internal combustion engine (ICE) cars, as witnessed in recent partnerships, in a shift towards “reverse JVs”.

➤The recent Stellantis and Tata Technologies JV helps the foreign car maker diversify product development, as it builds on the Indian platform for over 50 countries.

➤India’s first wave of auto partnerships in the 1980s were built around foreign entity's terms, with India benefitting from the technology and the global partner from scale in the country.

➤The case for partnerships is urgent as European automakers face pressure from declining share in home markets due to Chinese brands, and shrinking export market in China as local players eat into their share.

📉Is inflation eating into the country’s growth story?

Reserve Bank of India’s latest monetary policy raised the forecast for growth and inflation. The irony of higher inflation potentially dragging growth is not lost on anyone, let alone the RBI which has said that there is some evidence of elevated inflationm though only limited sign of supply-side pressures. However, it might be difficult to distinguish this from second-round effect of inflation.

➤The RBI said that while there is limited evidence of demand-side pressures, risks in view of strong growth in monetary and credit aggregates exist.

➤Experts say that the 25 bps rate-hike announced by the RBI is just a precautionary step to manage inflatition expectations.

➤Headline Consumer Price Index has remained above 4 percent for the fourth straight month, above the RBI target, as food and fuel inflation continued.

🎬Soul searching, at a theatre near you

Indians are discovering bhakti at the Box Office with a string of low-budget devotional films like Laalo and Hanuman Ansh driving massive returns. This harks back to the era of success of Jai Santoshi Maa in 1975 – indicating the ever-green interest in well-told stories on the big screen, which attracts the audience over tele-serials.

➤Industry experts observe that there is a deep-seated market for religious and devotional stories which cuts across geographies, age groups, and segments in India.

➤In addition to the small-and mid-budget films, there are big releases including the multi-starrer Ramayanaa hitting the screens later this year, and Tera Sai, Rishabh Shetty’s Jai Hanuman and others scheduled to release in 2027.

➤Religious and devotional films are not new as people are used to seeing similar content on TV. Big screens bring excitement and curiosity to the audience as a lot relies on storytelling.
