Hello and welcome back to Forbes India Weekly Digest!

The season of conferences is upon us – soon after the Global Fintech Festival in Mumbai, the 18th BRICS Summit will conclude on Sunday.

The year marks 20 years of establishment of the bloc, which now has 11 member countries, including Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa, representing 49 percent of the global population. The theme of the summit under India’s chairship this year is “Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability” which assumes greater significance during this period of global unrest.

Beyond the spectacle, and commute woes, the summit holds special significance in the backdrop of the ongoing West Asia crisis. Iran’s President Masoud Pezeshkian on his first visit to India after assuming office, met Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Friday. Energy security amid the ongoing conflict, and safe passage of vehicles carrying supplies through the Strait of Hormuz topped the discussion between the two leaders.

The summit also marks the first visit of Chinese President Xi Jinping to India after the Galwan Valley stand-off of 2020. Add to the mix China’s close ally Russia, with which it shares limitless bilateral engagement and deepening defence relationship, and we have a mathematical three-body problem. Russian President Vladimir Putin sharing stage with Modi and Jinping also sends a message to the US about India’s ability to balance relationship with multiple nation-states, while assuming a neutral stance.

While the leaders of the global south engage on “dialogue and diplomacy way forward” as part of the New Delhi agreement for BRICS, here is all that unfolded over the week:

🏃Amazon’s race to catch up with quick commerce

Quick commerce is replacing ecommerce, and Amazon India has taken notice. The platform entered the 10-minute delivery race in January 2025 and has already scaled to 700 micro fulfilment centres across 50 cities with its Amazon Now offering. Though it competes with incumbents, Blinkit, Instamart, Zepto, and close rival Flipkart Minutes, Amazon’s USP lies in its ecommerce network and scale, in addition to deep pockets for investment.

😎Rising costs of entourage accompanying stars

It isn’t just the star who commands a premium on the movie sets. Producers point out the rising cost of entourage, with spot-boys of top actors often out-earning the best MBAs in the country. What used to be a two-to-three-member team has ballooned to a retinue of 20 to 30 people accompanying the star, with specific requirements for food and travel. The cost for paying the entourage often comes up to Rs 20 lakh per day, making it a cause for concern.

💰RBI now has a problem of plenty with liquidity

After a liquidity crunch, RBI now has a problem of plenty. The central bank shut down the Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) or FCNR(B) special window a month earlier than planned on August 31, as it reached $127.2 billion. The surplus is not without benefits as it strengthens Balance of Payments and the country’s forex reserves.

➤With the new flow, liquidity surplus has reached Rs 9.7 trillion, much higher than RBI’s preferred levels at 1 percent of net demand and time liabilities at Rs 2.7 trillion.

➤RBI could lean on instruments like longer-tenor VRRRs and sell-buy FX swaps, as well as long-term measures like OMO sales and temporary CRR increase to minimise the cost and durability of liquidity withdrawal.

➤The inflow from FCNR(B) deposits is credit positive for banks as it supplements deposit mobilisation and will help contain the increase in banks’ loan-to-deposit ratios (LDRs).

👗Indian crafts command the global stage

Chief creative officer of Fendi, Maria Grazia Chiuri and textile house conserving artisanal legacy Chanakya International have come up with their special collaboration, ‘En Route’ to Mumbai. The exhibition commissioned by the Vatican Apostolic Library in 2025 for the Jubilee Year, it features embroidered maps, handcrafted globes, two new embroidered maps of Mumbai and more.

➤The exhibition translates the stories of remarkable women who travelled the world when it was rare, through embroidery and textiles.

➤One of the projects created by women from the Chanakya School and Foundation, titled Antarik Vishwa captures the idea of the world as seen by these women who have limited means to travel.

➤The partnership between Chiuri and Karishma Swali, creative director of Chanakya International and the Chanakya School of Craft dates to the 1990s, with frequent collaborations over the years to bring Indian embroidery and craftsmanship to internationally luxury brands like Dior and Fendi.

🔀Coforge faces trouble at the top

The resignation of OP Bhatt, chairman and independent director of mid-cap IT company Coforge, brought out board-related governance issues in the company. An internal audit report by KPMG revealed that Bhatt had concealed negative performance reviews related to his tenure from other board members. However, market analysts said the issue was manageable at a company level, without impacting its market performance.
