Hello and welcome back to Forbes India Weekly Digest!

As the dust settles on the intent to overhaul testing and eligibility criteria for professional courses with the Public Examinations Amendment Bill, here comes the curveball; Indian IT sector is not generating new jobs.

This doesn’t come as a surprise, though. The Indian IT sector which employs more than six million people and is the largest white-collar employment generator in the country has been facing headwinds from rising costs of AI adoption, including token costs, as well as infrastructure payments. The pressure on the sector became more apparent when Co-founder and chief scientist at Zoho Corporation, Sridhar Vembu, voiced this on social media platform X.

Vembu pointed out an interesting aspect – while we continue to churn out engineering graduates, net new job creation by the IT sector has come down. Indian IT companies too have been cautious in their approach to hiring, with IT bellwether TCS reducing campus hiring targets from 40,000-plus in FY25 and FY 26, down to 20,000 in FY27.

With the increased adoption of agentic AI to write software, a lot of repetitive jobs are getting automated. Coupled with rising token costs, tightened spends by enterprises due to global unrest, and negotiations on billing, the Indian IT sector has had to evaluate how can they supplement their core business with additional avenues of income which will sustain over the long-term, with the addition of data centres, and other capabilities. On the flipside, data centres do not generate jobs in bulk and can function as “lights-off” centres.

However, industry experts hope that this slump corrects itself over the next two years, as AI application spreads to new sectors like healthcare, BFSI etc., which will then step up to hire engineering talent to run these operations.

Here is hoping for the best.

💸Rupee continues to be under pressure

The central bank’s thought process on easing the pressure on the Rupee included capital account boosting measures announced in June. However, the Rupee continues to be vulnerable due to global headwinds and continued demand for dollars. With the Monetary Policy Committee’s meeting to conclude later this week, all eyes are on RBI for the next steps.

✦As of July 17, banks have mobilised a cumulative $20.7 bn worth of flows, of which $17.4 bn was under FCNR (B) deposit scheme.

✦As majority deposits under FCNR (B) schemes come up for renewal by September under the new rules, it is likely to attract investors.

✦The pipeline of fresh inflows has slowed down in mid-July due to higher global dollar rates narrowing bond yields and the tax treatment of interest income.

👔AI will transform the IT sector jobs, not replace them

The number of people employed by the Indian IT sector hasn’t kept pace with the growth of the sector itself, admits Nasscom President Rajesh Nambiar. However, the slump is likely to last for a shorter term as AI deployment goes beyond IT and ITeS into new fields such as healthcare, agriculture, etc which will open new avenues.

✦AI will automate tasks, and not jobs, leading to a recalibration of job roles.

✦The impact on employment is being felt largely by the middle layer of employees in the IT-ITeS sector who are unable to reskill themselves to match up to the new roles.

✦Lending context to data and cleaning it is a large services opportunity where India can lead.

🏃Hyrox is off to a running start in India

Hyrox has taken over the global fitness industry by storm, given the challenge it offers and the ability to cut across age, gender, and geographic divide. The hybrid fitness competition which combines 16 tasks debuted in India in 2025 and has already conducted five races across the country.

✦From a participation of 1600 contenders during its India debut in 2025, the competition expects 15,000 people to participate in the upcoming race in September in Mumbai.

✦Started by Christian Toetzke and Moritz Furste in Leipzig Germany, the competition is likely to see 2 million participants worldwide in 2026.

✦The sport is also targeting inclusion in Olympics by 2032, another reason why gyms and brands are willing to hop on the Hyrox wagon.
