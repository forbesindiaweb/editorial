The good news first – India’s GDP (Gross Domestic Product) touched 7.8 percent for the quarter ending June 30, 2026.

And now for the bad news – a former bureaucrat, a former chief statistician, and the political opposition have questions about the veracity of the number given the recent changes in the way the economic growth indicator is measured.

GDP is a measure of production in the country and indicative of whether the economy of a country is expanding or shrinking. The government introduced the new series of GDP estimates in February 2026, citing better capture of the progress of the economy.

Apart from revising the base year for calculation of GDP from 2011-22 to 2022-23, the new series also considers updated data sources, methodological changes, coverage of emerging sectors such as digital, platform, and gig economy, and application of supply and use table framework.

The major contention around the 7.8 percent growth has been the lowering of GDP numbers for Q1 FY26 by Rs 6 trillion. The use of Producer Price Index (PPI)—which excludes indirect tax and imports—in the calculations instead of Consumer Price Index and Wholesale Price Index, is another reason for discontent.

The numbers for the quarter were driven by growth in the tertiary sector such as financial, real estate, IT, and professional services segment, followed by secondary sector growth in manufacturing, utilities and construction.

Analysts have also raised the full-year estimate for GDP in FY27 from 6.8 percent to 7 percent, after the Q1 numbers were announced.

🚗Festive cheer for automakers

Automakers like Maruti, Tata, and Hyundai reported strong growth for the month of August, driven by festive season demand. However, the pace of growth in the coming months is unlikely to match up to the surge in vehicle purchase due to GST rate cuts last year.

➤Passenger vehicle makers dispatched 4.5 lakh units in August, a year-on-year growth of 36 percent, ahead of the festive season.

➤The growth comes over last year’s weak base of 3.3 lakh units, due to a fall in purchase in anticipation of a cut in GST rates.

➤The two-wheeler segment also logged strong gains ahead of the festive season.

🎩Indian private banks face leadership vacuum

There has been a leadership vacuum at Indian private banks as they find it difficult to fill top leadership spots, as witnessed recently by HDFC CEO declining the offer for re-appointment. It is not due to the lack of capable talent but related to corporate governance issues and boardroom disagreements.

➤Weakness in internal controls, risk management or board-management dynamics impact leadership continuity at banks.

➤The RBI mandates that banks initiate the reappointment process of a CEO at least six months ahead of the incumbent’s term expires, while a new CEO proposal must be submitted four months ahead.

➤Easing the obligations of CEOs instead of holding them responsible for every lapse in statutory filings might make it easier for the banks to fill the gap.

🍭Lowered sugar production spikes price

Despite predictions of record sugarcane production in May, sugar prices kept rising over the last few weeks, before settling at an average retail price of Rs 64.24 per kg on August 30, up from Rs 46.30 per kg in the year ago period. The record amount of sugarcane has been translated to record production due to various factors.

➤The national average recovery rate—the amount of sugar that can be extracted from a given quantity of cane—fell this season to 8.9 percent from 9.7 percent.

➤Government attributed the decline to sugarcane crop diseases, reducing the quality of the crop, and waterlogging caused by excess rainfall.

➤Contrary to popular opinion, ethanol blending for E20 petrol does not compete with domestic food security and is not a factor in recent price hikes.
