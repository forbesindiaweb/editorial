Hello and welcome back to Forbes India Weekly Digest!

If you are in Delhi, chances are you spent the weekend cleaning up the house for Diwali and dusting out the air purifier. Mumbaiites are sharing the experience as the AQI teeters towards unhealthy, and the rest of India is ringing in the festive cheer.

The week-long COP 31 faces the uphill task of brokering international climate cooperation in the current geopolitical scenario, even as developing nations demand fairer treatment and clear agenda on the cards to protect their interests. Climate funding will be a key concern as inflationary pressure and supply chain disruption due to the ongoing war have been the central problem to solve for governments worldwide.

Here is hoping for cleaner air, water, resources for the future generations, and freedom from cocooned festivities.

🚀The trajectory of India’s national space organisation ISRO

An increase in the number of private players in India's space programme and recent policy announcements have unsettled the employees at the national space agency ISRO. The Indian Space Policy of 2023 requires ISRO to transfer mature systems to industries and recent statements mention that the agency will stop making launch vehicles eventually. This has led to employees seeking clarification on the role of ISRO going ahead and whether the agency is likely to get privatised in the future.

📉Spike in global bond yields dragging Indian equities market

The two-decade high in global bond yields have dimmed the lure of developed markets, diverting capital flow away from them. This is encouraging global investors to move towards fixed income and away from emerging market equities, creating headwinds for the sector. In addition to these factors, supply shocks, rising global debt, and rising inflation have further added to market volatility.

🚍Tata Motors’ tech and global expansion rides on its acquisition strategy

The acquisition of Italy's Iveco Group by Tata Motors for €3.9 billion which closed recently, is among a long string of acquisitions by the Indian company. Tata Motors has been prolific in making acquisitions abroad for technology access and market expansion, with mixed outcomes.

➤Acquisitions of Daewoo Commercial Vehicle, Jaguar Land Rover from Ford Motor, and recently Iveco has been a key strategy for Tata Motors to expedite technology development and creating market presence.

➤After the demerger of the passenger vehicle and commercial vehicle verticals in 2025, Iveco is Tata Motors' first commercial vehicle acquisition, making it world's fourth-largest commercial vehicle maker by volume.

➤Iveco holds an 11 percent share in the EU truck market and is Europe's second-largest bus manufacturer, adding annual revenues of €22 billion to Tata Motors’ books.

🏦The IDBI bank sale is the latest in the push for bank privatisation

The recent approval for disinvestment of government-backed IDBI Bank which awards Canada's Fairfax Holding a 60.72 percent controlling stake in the entity for Rs 52,878 crore marks the blueprint for future strategic sales by the government.

➤The deal balances private sector-control with continued government participation with the government retaining 15 percent stake in the bank, while selling shares worth Rs 26,000 crore.

➤The deal also offers fresh momentum to India's privatisation agenda, though it remains dependent on strategic sales.

➤For Fairfax, the deal brings in an opportunity to create a global bank headquartered in India offering diversified financial services spanning insurance, wealth management, and investment banking.

📱Festive season fail to revive flagging mobile sales

Rising chip costs which have hit handsets priced below Rs 20,000 the worst, have led to a decline in mobile phone sales during the festive sales during Navratri and Dussehra. Sales fell 30 percent during the festivities, compared to last year, with Diwali being the last hope to recover sales.

➤The consistent rise in smartphone prices through the year is dissuading buyers from upgrading to new phones, while also impacting discounts offered by online and offline channels.

➤Top five smartphone OEMs have indicated that post-Diwali entry-level 5G smartphones will now be priced in the Rs 25,000 instead of Rs 15,000, pushing it out of reach for budget conscious consumers.

➤iPhone inventory supply to mainline retail markets has been low for the 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max models.
