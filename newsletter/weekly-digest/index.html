As the world battles AI-anxiety from taking away their employment, the US administration sprung up another surprise for tech firms in the country looking to hire foreign talent.

US Vice President JD Vance suspended processing of applications for Permanent Labour Certification (PERM) by eight technology firms, including the top four Indian IT firms, on Thursday. The PERM process is a precursor to the process of obtaining an employer sponsored green card, which proves that the number and availability of qualified individuals for a particular job profile in the US can only be filled by hiring a qualified foreign individual and will not impact the employment and remuneration of US citizens working in similar roles.

Vance alleged that the tech firms were misusing the provisions under H-1B visa which allows non-immigrants to work in the US, and the permanent residence channels to hire “indentured servants” from other countries at lower pay. The US veep added that the government had unearthed misuse over decades, and that a foreign worker made $48,000 less (annually) than an American worker in the same role when brought in by a foreign outsourcing firm.

India’s Ministry of External Affairs has taken umbrage to the use of the term “indentured servants” for its colonial connotations, and for doing disservice to the skilled workers from India. The fact remains that first the steep H-1B visa fee which was raised to $100,000 for specialised workers coming to work as non-immigrants, and now the suspension of key tech firms is likely to hit the margins of the IT industry in the long-term.

The solution might lie in offshore and global operation delivery, while expanding local hiring for Indian IT firms to mitigate the impact.

📞With AI use, customer service must balance empathy and data

Customers are rejecting bad after-sales service and query resolution experiences – whether they interact with a chatbot or a human service provider. It has become imperative for brands to balance AI-led efficiency with empathy to ensure continued engagement and repeat orders.

➤An average Indian consumer loses 10.8 hours a year trying to resolve service-related issues, making poor service a measurable revenue risk for brands.

➤Customer service directly influences retention, brand advocacy, repeat purchase, and overall business growth.

➤Indian consumers are increasingly demanding convenient and seamless interaction across interfaces. Companies need to use AI to elevate the experience rather than a mere cost control measure.

💰India’s micro-lenders tighten underwriting to survive

After one of the worst downturns for the industry, India’s Micro Finance Institutions (MFIs) are making a comeback with stricter underwriting norms and diversified offerings to hedge their risks. The post-pandemic bust was characterised by exposure of MFIs to customers who were borrowing from multiple institutions. This has served as a cautionary tale for the industry against over-leveraging.

➤The same customer can borrow from a fintech, NBFC, or bank outside the MFI framework or informal lenders, raising indebtedness and hitting collections.

➤MFIs are countering the risk by being repeat lenders to existing customers through their life-stage rather than chasing new borrowers.

➤Change in RBI guidelines in 2025 has helped NBFC-MFIs to diversify 40 percent of their loans outside microfinance, compared to 25 percent earlier.

📚The hamster wheel keeps spinning even after cracking JEE

The human-cost of cracking the JEE for entry into the prestigious IITs continues even after the student is enrolled in the college. Faced by a competitive atmosphere, the goal to score well during the examinations and excel in campus placements stacks on pressure on students already exhausted by years of intensive coaching.

➤In 2026, 15.38 lakh students appeared for the JEE with only 56,880 qualifying in the final round. The preparation for the competitive examination starts in early school years.

➤The hardest realisation for students when they join an IIT is that they are one among many who have consistently excelled in academic performance.

➤Competition within the IITs extends to scoring a good CGPA which determines eligibility for internships and placements.
