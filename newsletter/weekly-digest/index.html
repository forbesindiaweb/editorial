It’s football season and the face-off of the legends is just around the corner. The internet also reminds you that the oldest players in the world cup are in their 40s and are playing their last World Cup. The end of an era!

There is a new chapter in American Football though; an ownership group led by Indian-origin tech billionaire, entrepreneur, and venture capitalist Vinod Khosla bought reigning Super Bowl champions, Seattle Seahawks, for $9.6 billion from Paul Allen’s estate!

The National Football League (NFL) owners are likely to ratify the final purchase agreement in August.

Since the demise of former Microsoft CEO Allen in 2018, the estate has been working towards divesting his investments in sports franchises. His investment in National Basketball Association’s team, Portland Trail Blazers, was sold to an investment group led by Carolina Hurricanes owner Tom Dundon in September 2025.

The Khosla family will become the team’s controlling owner, according to reports. This includes his wife Neeru Khosla, with a combined net worth of $13.7 billion. Khosla, 71, was the co-founder of Sun Microsystems and is known for Khosla Ventures, a successful venture capital firm in the Silicon Valley which has backed companies like Stripe, DoorDash, Square, Robinhood and OpenAI, among others.

Khosla joins the list of tech billionaires who have invested in sports franchises in the US – including the likes of Steve Ballmer (NBA – Los Angeles Clippers) and Mark Cuban (NBA – Dallas Mavericks), among others.

🚘 EVs are changing the Indian car market

Electric Vehicles are increasingly becoming the primary car at home for Indians who have typically been ICE users. From policy changes, improved technology, to the global macro-economic conditions which have led fuel prices to spiral, it is the perfect storm for buyers to make the switch. By 2035, nearly 35 percent of all passenger four-wheelers sold will be electric, according to a report by KPMG.

🔌Flex-fuel push is driving consumers towards EV

Pump-anxiety and communication gap on ethanol blending is pushing Indian consumers to opt for EVs instead, where the policies are clearer. The mandate for E20 (20 percent ethanol-blended fuel) usage since April has buyers confused, with social media messaging adding to it.

➤EV penetration in India breached 12 percent in June, a 63 percent jump from the comparable time in the previous year.

➤Concerns on mileage reduction, engine health, and vehicle performance due to the use of ethanol-blended fuel is making buyers opt for EVs.

➤The policymakers have been pushing to ramp up flex-fuel and increasing the blending from E20 to E25, creating panic.

☕An iced cup of joe is the drink of choice now

Chai is no longer the official national fuel as younger generation, and millennials shift to iced specialty coffees. The specialty coffee market in the country is growing at a steady pace, with private capital flowing into players operating different formats, from kiosks to good old retail. However, the chief money-maker for most brands still remains food and other beverages.

➤India’s out-of-home coffee market is set to double to $3.5 billion-plus by 2030 according to estimates by research agency Redseer Consulting.

➤Coffee prices are moving towards premium with mid-market segment, priced at Rs 100 to Rs 200 per cup growing at nearly 40 percent.

➤Non-coffee F&B continues to contribute 30 percent or more to the topline for coffee chains, with iced drinks being favoured by the buyers.

📱Who is BSNL’s Rs 1.34 lakh satellite phone for?

Global Satellite phone service for civilians was launched by BSNL in 2018, though current draft leaves out Non-Geostationary Orbit (NGSO) operators – which would compete with BSNL including the likes of Starlink, Etusalat OneWeb, and Jio Satellite Communications.
