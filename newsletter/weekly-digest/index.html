As India steps into a series of festivals, starting with Teej, Onam, Raksha Bandhan, and Janmashtami, rising price of sugar might leave a bitter after-taste.

The price of sugar across retail and wholesale markets has increased by nearly 40 percent compared to the previous month, as well as the corresponding period last year. The average retail price of the commodity rose from Rs 48.18 per kg on July 20, 2026, to Rs 55.70 per kg as of August 20, 2026.

There has been a circle of blame doing the rounds, with the opposition holding the government responsible for failing to control the price rise and citing diversion of sugarcane for ethanol production. The government has said that the price rise has more to do with weather-related impact on the sugarcane crop and rise in input costs for farmers and mills, impacting the production. However, farmer’s groups say that the reduced stocks and increase in prices have more to do with hoarding by big traders.

The government has clarified that the share of sugar diverted for ethanol production has declined from 12 percent in FY23 to 9 percent in FY26, adding that India had enough stocks to last till the new crushing season set to begin in October.

The government has also allowed duty-free import of 10 LMT (lakh metric tons) of raw sugar to make up for a gap in production.

The great-Indian sweet tooth might have to wait a little longer for prices to stabilise.

📈India Inc’s Q1 FY27 earnings sail through on austerity

India’s corporate companies managed to clock strong earnings in Q1 FY27 by adopting austerity measures in the face of global headwinds and currency shocks. The aggregate net profit of listed companies improved by 23.7 percent in Q1, compared to a growth of 7.3 percent in the corresponding quarter in 2026.

➤Supply chain restructuring, disciplined inventory control, and selective price hikes, helped the companies preserve profitability.

➤The earnings of India Inc were weighed down by oil marketing companies facing elevated crude oil prices since the start of conflict in West Asia.

➤Nifty companies delivered an aggregate of 18 percent net profit growth, with the top five companies accounting for 60 percent of incremental year-on-year earnings.

💵Sowing season leads to drop in rural job scheme numbers

The new Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) or VB-G RAM G rural employment scheme by the government saw a drop of 49.94 percent drop in number of person-days for July 2026, compared to July 2025. VB-G RAM G replaced the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme (MGNREGS) on July 1, 2026. MGNREGS has been the government-backed rural-employment scheme for nearly two decades.

➤The fall in number of person-days coincides with the Kharif sowing season for crops when people usually are employed on the field instead of government schemes.

➤The drop can be attributed to a revised list of beneficiaries, as well as the 60-day pause period invoked by a handful of states.

➤If the numbers continue to decline over the long-term, it might dent rural demand.
