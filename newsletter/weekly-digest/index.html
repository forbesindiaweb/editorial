What’s in a (user)name? The possibility of impersonation, frauds, phishing and a lot more, if you ask the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).

Meta-owned messaging and call platform WhatsApp came up with the feature of picking a unique username for those using the platform as an identifier in lieu of phone numbers last week. The username will be displayed as “@Userame” when an unknown person contacts someone over a call or text on WhatsApp, masking their phone number.

The immediate benefit is reducing spam and protecting privacy if you are part of WhatsApp groups. However, it also digs up the old traceability debate where the government of India mandates traceability for Significant Social Media Intermediaries (with over 5 million users) to offer a mechanism to trace the point of origination of text or speech to curb spread of misinformation.

The IT Rules, 2021 mandate traceability which seems to be at the centre of a notice sent by MeitY to Meta, asking it to address concerns on identity spoofing, and possibility of increase in online fraud, phishing, digital arrest scams and impersonation.

Meta has time till July 9 to come back with its response. While the username feature is global, India is the first country to have asked Meta to hold back on the roll out.

Just like the “forward” arrows on texts being shared with multiple users, WhatsApp might come up with new features or markers for the username debate.

⏫Indian middle class powering growth says FM

India’s middle class has been the engine of growth rather than just being a byproduct of it, said Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman recently during a discussion. The post-Covid consumption by the strata has been a primary driving force of growth, said the minister.

➤India is expected to overtake China in terms of size of the middle class between 2030 and 2035.

➤Nearly 93 percent of all Indian consumer spending is expected to come from middle class and affluent households by 2036.

➤The rise in number of middle classes spans urban centres and beyond, with 500 Indian cities poised to become hubs of economic activity in the future.
