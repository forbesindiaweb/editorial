Hello and welcome back to Forbes India Weekly Digest!

The week saw the order of things change across two large conglomerates; one a little less dramatic than the other.

Tata Sons Chairman N Chandrasekaran resigned from the board of the private entity which controls Tata Group of companies, after waiting around for six months for an extension. He will complete his term in February 2027 and was the first Chairman-elect outside the Tata-Mistry family fold.

Given that Tata Trusts, the philanthropic arm of the family holds nearly 66 percent stake in Tata Sons and can nominate up to three directors, or one-third of the board of directors, the power balance is skewed in favour of Ratan Tata’s half-brother Noel Tata, who took over as the trust’s chairman after the former’s demise in 2024.

The Tata Sons’ board has also thinned down, with directors stepping down from their positions due to the stipulation of retirement age, bringing down the tally to six, including two independent directors. Three directors stepped down from the board in FY26 for various reasons.

If anything, N Chandrasekaran’s departure will change the direction the conglomerate takes going ahead, faced with rising losses from Air India and Tata Digital. The Annual General Meeting on August 18 will be closely watched for early signs of change.

In a separate development, Managing Director and CEO of Godrej Consumer Products (GCPL), Sudhir Sitapati also stepped down from his role. The company was quick to act, appointing Asif Malbari, the company’s global CFO, as his successor.

For the Tata saga, the successor has a tall order to fill, including the tug-of-war on whether Tata Sons should list on the bourses, and taking the cash-guzzling businesses in the conglomerate to the finish line.

🎢Top deck exits rock share price

The stocks of GCPL and Tata Group-related companies witnessed knee-jerk reaction from shareholders after the news of N Chandrasekaran’s exit from Tata Sons and Sudhir Sitapati’s resignation as MD and CEO of GCPL became public. The blue-chip companies saw immediate feedback on the indices with quick sell-offs mid-week.

➤Shares of GCPL tanked over 10 percent on BSE on August 12 during the day’s trade, plunging 9.69 percent over the five-day period as of today.

➤Sell-off in Tata Group companies were quick with IT company TCS trading 5 percent lower on August 12. The stocks have tanked nearly 5.31 percent over the five-day period as of today.

➤Tata Group has nearly 20 major companies spanning sectors like IT, steel, auto, and retail trading on BSE.
