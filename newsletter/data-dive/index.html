Casting a milkman as James Bond

Dear reader,

It was 007 all over our timelines this week. The announcement of Robert Pattinson as the new James Bond in Denis Villeneuve’s Bond 26, “broke the internet”.

If, at any point in the past six decades, you have watched even one of the 25 James Bond films (or all of them—in which case, hello legend!), then you already know why this is such a big deal.

Ever since 1962, when Sir Sean Connery a former milkman and the only Bond actor to win an Oscar, first played the superspy—the James Bond films always made the star. The star was never really cast as Secret Agent 007 until now.

So naturally our data deck this week opens with ‘Bond at the Box Office’ as Robert Pattinson inherits Bond 26 amid high expectations.

Other stories this week also cover premium street rents, a feature on art advisers guiding Indian collectors, states reversing two-child penalties, pricey gold failing to dull festive demand, and India’s rising bond yields.

In our multimedia section this week, we sat down with Jaipur Rugs’ NK Chaudhary to discuss craftsmanship and entrepreneurship.

Until next time!

-Samreen

 
Graphic of the Week Bond at the Box Office

Robert Pattinson steps into Bond 26 carrying hefty expectations. The franchise’s modern era dwarfs its past. Daniel Craig’s Skyfall grossed $1.11 billion worldwide in 2012, the only 007 film to cross that mark. Yet momentum faded, with Spectre at $879 million and No Time to Die at $759 million. Even so, all five Craig-era hits outearned every pre-2002 Bond film, none of which topped $500 million. Figures are not inflation-adjusted, so early classics like Goldfinger and Thunderball look smaller than their true heft. Graphic by Mukesh Singh
