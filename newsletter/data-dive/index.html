Did History’s First Stock Pay Off?

Dear reader,

For some small investors, not really.

You perhaps already know that the Dutch East India Company (or VOC) was the first company ever to list publicly in 1602, just three years before they set up their first Indian factory at Masulipatnam.

Among its earliest investors was Neeltgen Cornelis, a domestic worker for VOC founder Dirck van Os. Earning less than 50 cents a day, she scraped together 100 guilders, or more than 200 days’ worth of wages, to buy into the historic IPO.

Unfortunately, history suggests that her brave bet didn’t pay off.

Like many small investors then and now, Neeltgen ran out of cash. The company required payments in multiple instalments, which she couldn’t afford, forcing her to sell her shares early. To make matters worse, the VOC didn’t pay its first dividend until 1610, leaving her with little to no profit from history’s most famous IPO.

By contrast, the record listing of Jio Platforms underscores the power of modern retail liquidity. Opening at Rs 1,358 per share, a 21 percent premium, Jio immediately created value for the public investors who lined up for its debut. Besides the Jio IPO, this week we capture other largest IPO listings in India.

Our lineup is also populated by the space tech sector this time. Check out our stories on how India’s nascent private space tech sector is already winning international orders while ISRO turns the page to its next era.

And before you leave, be sure to catch our cover story podcast, where Himani and I discuss what’s next for the sector.

Until next time!
-Samreen
