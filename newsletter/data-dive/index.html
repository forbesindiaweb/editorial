An affair that cost a throne

Dear reader,

Power has always been vulnerable to private scandal, from Buckingham Palace to Silicon Valley.

Those among you who have binged The Crown or watched The King’s Speech will know of the reluctant, stuttering monarch “Bertie”, better known as King George VI, the father of Queen Elizabeth II.

But did you know that he only wore the crown because his older brother, King Edward VIII, abdicated the throne to marry an American socialite? It caused quite a scandal back in the day, ultimately costing him his throne and place in the royal establishment.

Nearly a century later, Silicon Valley witnessed its own royal abdication this week. Jensen Huang spent over three decades building Nvidia into the world’s most valuable company (it no longer holds that distinction), only to watch his reign implode in a single week.

As I write this, a third affair has come to light. Regardless of whether you believe that personal lives should or shouldn’t have corporate consequences, history shows they often do. Read our story on how the personal lives of several tycoons have carried a corporate cost.

For more data-driven coverage this week, we look at how layoffs are mounting in 2026 as joblessness among rural women deepens, how India’s fiscal deficit has hit a five-year high, and how more firms are paying directors the maximum Rs 1 lakh sitting fee.

And if you are studying at ALLEN Career Institute, know somebody who is, or are hearing about it for the first time, I am sure there is something for you in our interview with its founder, Rajesh Maheshwari, who talks about building one of India’s most influential education institutions.

Until next time!
—Samreen

Qualcomm’s reported plan to cut 1,000 jobs in India would join home-interiors startup Livspace at the top of 2026 layoff list. CorroHealth (800) and Innodata (550) follow, while Flipkart cut 500 jobs, Paytm 400, Even Healthcare 350, Zomato and Opendoor 250 each, and PayPal 220. Qualcomm’s figure rests on media reports and is unconfirmed by the company.
