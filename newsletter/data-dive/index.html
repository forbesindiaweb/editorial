She challenged CV Raman’s bias, and won

Dear reader,

Science requires intellect. Success demands grit. But in the 1930s, Kamala Sohonie needed more than that to get into the Indian Institute of Science (IISc). Sohonie’s research fellowship to IISc was rejected by the formidable Nobel laureate CV Raman, the then director of the Institute, only because she was a woman and hence thought of as less competent to pursue research.

It took a satyagraha outside his office, an extended probation and a patronising warning “not to be a distraction to male colleagues” for Sohonie to become the first woman to be admitted to IISc. She endured it all to also become the first woman to complete her Master’s with distinction from IISc in 1936. Her sheer competence changed the institution forever, compelling Prof. Raman to open IISc to women.

We have come a long way since the 1930s. Our graphic roundup this week shows how women’s STEM enrolment has climbed to 44 percent in 2023-24, led by sharp PhD gains.

And as India’s UK FTA comes into force this week, we look at how India’s trade deficit with several of its other FTA partners is widening. While the clouds of war may be looming over West Asia again, it is the monsoon (or rather the delay in it) that is concerning analysts here at home. Also check out our coverage of the new services sector index and AI trends in GCC hiring.

And if you have booked your seats for the epic The Odyssey this weekend, we have some news for you. Despite paying premium ticket prices, unfortunately you won’t experience The Odyssey the way Christopher Nolan intended. Why? Because India has none of the 41 theatres worldwide capable of screening an IMAX 70mm film. Check out our graphic slideshow for more such data nuggets on Nolan’s most expensive film to date.

A Huma Qureshi interview rounds out our multimedia pick this week.

Until next time!

Samreen

 
Graphic of the Week Women gain in STEM

Women’s enrolment in science, technology, engineering and mathematics courses or STEM touched 44 percent in 2023-24, according to a recent report by the ministry of education. Female participation in STEM education is steadily climbing across India, with PhD enrolment showing the sharpest gains, up from 41.4 percent in 2021-22 to 46.7 percent in 2023-24. Postgraduate courses already have a female majority at 57 percent, while undergraduate enrolment has inched past 42 percent in 2023-24. The trend signals a widening pipeline of women moving into advanced research roles, even as undergraduate numbers show room to grow.
