Neil Armstrong was sceptical of this idea

Dear reader,

It wasn’t that long ago that Neil Armstrong expressed some scepticism about relying entirely on the private aerospace industry for human spaceflight. In his 2010 congressional testimony, while he supported the “encouragement of the newcomers toward their goal of lower cost access to space”, the first person to walk on the moon cautioned—based on his experience and conversations with veteran engineers—that developing safe and reliable human-rated spacecraft would take significant time and funding.

Sixteen years later, Elon Musk has proven those doubts wrong. He is not just the richest man on earth today, but SpaceX’s stock market performance is now the envy of the entire business world. India’s private spacetech industry is aiming for that same blend of commercial success and scientific breakthrough.

Now that Skyroot, an Indian private spacetech company has just successfully sent a rocket to orbit, this week we cover how the race is on to build one that comes back—quite like what Musk has done with Falcon-9.

Also check our stories on how RCB’s brand value jumped 60 percent and crossed $300 million after its maiden title defence against Gujarat Titans. Elsewhere, government VC investments hit a record $61.9 million in 2026, surpassing 2024 and 2025, while India’s electronics exports gained momentum on rising iPhone and smartphone shipments.

And if you are travelling this season and looking for a piece of luggage that reflects your style and personality, read our story on the luxury luggage brand Nappa Dori, born in a Delhi garage, which now counts Hollywood stars as clients.

Until next time!
Samreen

 
Graphic of the Week Royal Returns

Royal Challengers Bengaluru’s (RCB) brand value climbed 60 percent to $312 million between 2023 and 2026, as per Houlihan Lokey’s valuation data, the steepest jump among all Indian Premier League (IPL) franchises, after Punjab Kings’ 75.6 percent rise. The re-rating followed RCB’s maiden title defence in the franchise’s 18-year history, sealed with a five-wicket win over Gujarat Titans in Ahmedabad. Mumbai Indians (38.9 percent) and Kolkata Knight Riders (35.4 percent) trailed, while Chennai Super Kings logged the slowest growth at 15.1 percent despite topping 2023’s valuations.
