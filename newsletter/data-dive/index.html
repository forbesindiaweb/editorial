How weed launched online shopping

Dear reader,

Perhaps e-commerce history isn’t the first thing that strikes you when hitting ‘Buy Now’ on Amazon, Flipkart, or Myntra. But a bag of weed was actually the first item ever bought or sold online. No one knows the exact quantity exchanged except the Stanford AI Lab students who placed the order with students from MIT in the early 1970s on an earlier version of the internet called Arpanet. And just like you, they probably weren’t thinking about the history or future of e-commerce either.

Now, whether you support or oppose weed, use is surging among older Americans. A recent JAMA Internal Medicine study reveals marijuana use among seniors (65 years and above) jumped nearly 46 percent between 2021 and 2023, with the sharpest rise seen among the wealthy.

Unsurprisingly, cannabis dealmaking is also heating up once again. The resurgence in M&A activity is headlined by Curaleaf’s unsolicited bid for Aurora and Vireo Growth’s swift play for distressed assets, according to media reports. In fact, American companies are also mounting an aggressive push across Europe through major M&As. All of this made us look at the variance in usage of cannabis across countries. While Canada leads at 26 percent and the US reaches 21.9 percent, adoption remains low in Asia, with India reporting just 2.8 percent usage.

But if you would rather read about the much-debated GDP figures this week, check out our coverage of how the Q1FY27 GDP numbers beat most analyst estimates on the back of manufacturing, services and investment activity.

Elsewhere, our data roundup this week captures a widening IT salary gap; independent film festivals struggling for funding even as mainstream cinema thrives; and Indian remittances from the US facing pressure from H-1B visa curbs just as Gulf-linked inflows soften.

And with our special Showstopper 2026 edition now on stands, check out our multimedia coverage starting with our podcast which gives a BTS on the issue alongside an interview with India’s new face of food security—FDA Commissioner Tukaram Mundhe.

Until next time!
-Samreen
