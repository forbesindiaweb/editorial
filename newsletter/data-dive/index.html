Chasing launches but getting it all wrong

Dear reader,

In a Hyderabad meeting last year with Sanjay Nekkanti, one of the three co-founders and CEO of space tech startup Dhruva Space, I brought up China’s dominant launch count and asked if matching those numbers should be India’s goal.
Now, let me just explain the context of the question before I get to what he said in response. China already had over 72 space launches till November—from what I read in many media reports. Isro had fewer than 10.

Now to what Nekkanti said. He believed that with evolving demand for space products and services and with scaling up of space infra, the number of launches in India would only go up in the future. “It depends on what are the needs of your own country, and how do you solve for your country’s needs first. And today, I believe that we are at a stage where we are creating category defining products that will not just be leaders for India, but also the global markets,” he added.

This was before Skyroot became India’s first space tech unicorn to launch a rocket in space, before GalaxEye launched its breakthrough OptoSAR satellite, or Pixxel secured its record funding round. I am sure these developments would have thrilled Nekkanti, especially since Dhruva has also bagged Rs 105 crore in RDIF government funding for Project Garud in the past year.

And if you are tempted to Google what each of these space missions achieved, chances are you will stumble upon stories from our graphic deck this week which talk about Indian space tech startups making global headlines this year. We also covered ISRO’s successful EOS-05 launch last weekend, which earned India entry into an elite satellite club. And beyond deep-tech, alternative fuel vehicles are spreading past traditional auto hubs, while SEBI’s curbs have triggered a decade-first decline in retail F&O traders.

We also interviewed Sabyasachi Mukherjee this week, where he discusses his Met Museum collaboration, craftsmanship and retail philosophy.

Until next time!
-Samreen
