OpenAI engineer allegedly used chatbot to plan office surveillance

An OpenAI engineer allegedly used a chatbot to help plan a covert recording operation inside the company’s San Francisco offices, WIRED reported, citing a criminal complaint. Prosecutors allege he placed a listening device in a conference room used by senior executives and queried an internal tool about meetings there. OpenAI discovered the device during a security sweep and notified the FBI. The employee was arrested and fired. OpenAI said it had no indication that he had improperly accessed or extracted model weights, source code or customer data. His motive remains unclear.

AI agents accessed Indian firms’ sensitive data undetected: CERT-In

AI models and agents accessed Indian organisations’ live systems without authorisation during research and evaluation, India’s cybersecurity agency CERT-In has warned. They read business records, personal data and confidential material by exploiting exposed credentials and insecure configurations, according to an advisory, reported Business Standard. None of the affected organisations detected the access; AI companies voluntarily disclosed the incidents. Separately, CERT-In identified financially motivated attacks using AI agents that compressed the time to impact from days to hours. The agency warned that AI was exploiting familiar security weaknesses faster and at greater scale, with incompletely fixed systems attacked again.

Nvidia expands AI push across enterprise agents and chipmaking

Nvidia has unveiled new AI models and software tools spanning enterprise automation and semiconductor manufacturing. Its Nemotron 3.5 lineup includes Super VL, a 120-billion-parameter model that interprets documents, charts and webpages, and smaller Bolt models for local and conversational agents. Partners including Salesforce and Atlassian are adapting Super VL for their applications. Separately, Nvidia introduced cuPotential, a tool for simulating atomic interactions that it says accelerated existing GPU-based workflows two- to threefold in early testing. Semiconductor companies including Applied Materials, Synopsys and Lam Research are using Nvidia’s technology to accelerate chip design, manufacturing simulations and inspection.

AI selloff hits hedge funds as doubts over spending mount

An unwinding of AI bets hit global hedge funds this week, with Asia-focused managers suffering the steepest losses, Reuters reported. Goldman Sachs data showed global stock-picking funds fell 2.9 percent in the week through October 15, although they remained up 14.3 percent this year. Asia-focused stock pickers lost 6.8 percent in October’s first half. Investors are questioning how quickly massive AI spending will deliver returns, while hotter-than-expected US inflation intensified fears of further rate hikes. Semiconductor-heavy markets suffered: South Korea’s Kospi fell 8 percent on Thursday, while Taiwan’s stocks declined more than 3 percent.

Meta expands AI lineup, teams up with Apple

Meta has launched Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, expanding its push into coding and AI assistants for everyday and workplace tasks. The models are designed to remember relevant details across chats and persist with complex, multistep assignments. Muse Wonder is available through Meta’s developer API and Muse Code, while Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers. In a subsequent announcement, Meta released Muse Spark Open, an open-weight model for coding and AI agents. It worked with Apple to optimise the model for Mac Studio computers, allowing developers to run it locally rather than rely on cloud services.
