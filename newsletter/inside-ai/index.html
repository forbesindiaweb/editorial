Hello reader,

A series of recent security incidents has exposed the risks that accompany increasingly capable and autonomous AI systems. OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta have disclosed instances in which models being tested for cybersecurity tasks gained unauthorised access to external systems—sometimes because of failures in controlled testing environments.

It raises a broader question: Is AI becoming both the problem and the solution in cybersecurity? The same models that can help companies detect threats and uncover vulnerabilities can also be used—or act—to exploit them. As their capabilities advance, building effective safeguards will become as important as building the technology itself.

Yet the AI race continues to gather momentum. This week, Nvidia mobilised capital for infrastructure, Microsoft and L&T expanded India’s computing capacity, Meta brought AI agents to laptops, Accel raised fresh funds, and DeepSeek moved deeper into robotics.

Meta brings AI agents to laptops

Meta has released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model designed for always-on AI agents, coding, function calling and other tool-based tasks. Distilled from the company’s larger Muse Spark model, it can operate on a Mac or PC using a single consumer GPU. Running the model locally could reduce dependence on cloud services, lower costs and give users greater control over sensitive data. The launch also signals Meta’s renewed push into open models as developers seek smaller, customisable systems suited to specific applications.

Nvidia plans $500 billion AI financing push

Nvidia has partnered with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to establish financing platforms for AI infrastructure. The initiative aims to mobilise more than $500 billion in third-party capital over time, creating dedicated pools of funding for Nvidia customers. The money could support data centres and other computing infrastructure required to train and operate AI systems. The arrangement also broadens Nvidia’s role beyond supplying chips, helping customers finance the expensive facilities that ultimately drive demand for its hardware and software.

Accel raises $3.5 billion for startup investments

Venture capital firm Accel has raised $3.5 billion across four new funds, giving it fresh capital to back startups across major technology markets. The total includes $800 million each for the US and Europe-Israel, $550 million for India and a $1.35 billion global expansion fund. Artificial intelligence is expected to remain a major investment theme, particularly at the application layer. For India, the new fund signals continued confidence in early-stage companies despite its smaller size compared with Accel’s previous India-focused vehicle.

DeepSeek makes a robotics bet

Chinese artificial intelligence company DeepSeek has invested $20.8 million in humanoid robot maker Unitree through the latter’s Shanghai initial public offering. The investment gives DeepSeek 2.31 percent of the shares allocated through the IPO’s strategic placement. Both companies are based in Hangzhou and plan to collaborate by combining DeepSeek’s AI models with Unitree’s expertise in mechanical engineering, motion control and robotics. The partnership could help DeepSeek move beyond language models into embodied intelligence, where machines must understand and act within physical environments.

L&T wins Rs 15,000 crore AI data-centre order

Larsen & Toubro has secured an order worth between Rs 10,000 crore and Rs 15,000 crore from US-based Together AI, marking its entry into the AI factory business. The company will deploy 10,000 Nvidia B300 GPUs at its Chennai data-centre campus to support large-scale model training, fine-tuning and inference. L&T describes the project as India’s largest single-cluster AI infrastructure facility. The contract highlights how Indian engineering companies are positioning themselves to participate in the capital-intensive buildout supporting global artificial intelligence workloads.

Microsoft opens its largest India data-centre hub

Microsoft has opened its largest data-centre region in India in Hyderabad, adding to its cloud regions in Pune, Chennai and Mumbai. Adani Group and HDFC Bank are among the first customers of the new facility, which brings Microsoft’s Indian footprint to four regions. The expansion is intended to meet rising demand for locally hosted cloud services, data storage and AI computing. It also forms part of Microsoft’s broader India push, backed by a planned $17.5 billion investment in cloud and artificial intelligence infrastructure through 2029.
