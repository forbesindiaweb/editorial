India’s semiconductor ambitions are entering a new phase. Under Semicon 2.0, the government plans to invest alongside venture capital firms by taking equity stakes in chip startups, before exiting those investments as the companies mature.

The model attempts to address a persistent challenge for semiconductor startups: Developing a chip is expensive, takes years and requires patient capital long before meaningful revenues appear. By matching private investments without seeking operational control, the government hopes to reduce the risk for investors while allowing founders to retain control.

The move also expands India’s chip strategy beyond attracting large manufacturing projects. The Rs 1.28 lakh crore programme will support chip design, intellectual property, advanced packaging, materials, equipment and research—parts of the semiconductor ecosystem in which domestic startups could play a larger role.

The semiconductor push is one of several developments reflecting India’s attempt to build more of the AI value chain.

1️⃣ TCS plans new AI deployment workforce

Photo by Dhiraj Singh/Bloomberg via Getty Images

Tata Consultancy Services plans to create as many as 8,900 forward-deployed engineering roles to help customers integrate and customise AI systems. These engineers will work closely with clients to adapt AI tools to their specific operations and workflows.

The IT major is also evaluating acquisitions in AI, data security and cybersecurity. The moves indicate how India’s traditional IT services companies are reshaping their business models as automation and AI agents begin handling more routine technology work.

2️⃣ India expands semiconductor programme

Photo by Sam Panthaky / AFP

The Indian government has approved an additional ₹1.28 lakh crore for the next phase of its semiconductor programme. The capital will support domestic chip design and intellectual-property development, research and development, and the establishment of more fabrication facilities.

The expansion is intended to reduce India’s reliance on imported chips and build a more complete domestic semiconductor ecosystem. Local manufacturing capacity is becoming increasingly strategic as AI development drives demand for advanced chips and computing infrastructure.

3️⃣ China pitches a new global AI order

Photo by Kevin Frayer/Getty Images

President Xi Jinping has positioned China as a champion of open-source AI and wider access to the technology, particularly for developing economies. Beijing is also leading a new international AI cooperation organisation supported by 29 countries.

The initiative signals China’s ambition to influence not only the development of AI but also the international standards governing it. Its pitch offers the Global South an alternative to an AI ecosystem largely shaped by American companies and US technology policy.

4️⃣ OpenAI launches ChatGPT Work

Photo courtesy ChatGPT

OpenAI has launched ChatGPT Work, an AI agent designed to complete complex, multi-step projects across apps and files. Powered by GPT-5.6, it can create documents, presentations, spreadsheets, charts, analysis and web applications while maintaining continuity across longer assignments.

The launch marks OpenAI’s attempt to move ChatGPT beyond answering questions or generating individual pieces of content. The company increasingly wants the product to function as an end-to-end work platform capable of independently executing entire workflows.

5️⃣ Emergent becomes India’s latest AI unicorn

Photo courtesy Emergent

Bengaluru-based Emergent has raised $130 million in Series C funding, valuing the AI software-building startup at $1.5 billion. The company allows founders, entrepreneurs and other non-coders to create production-ready web and mobile applications using natural-language instructions.

Emergent reached unicorn status within a year of publicly launching its platform. The startup now reports an annual revenue run rate of $120 million, highlighting the rapid adoption of AI tools that make software development more accessible.

6️⃣ Moonshot launches model rivalling US players

Photo courtesy Moonshot AI

Chinese startup Moonshot AI has unveiled Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model with a context window of one million tokens. The model reportedly competes with leading systems from US companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic on coding, reasoning and other complex tasks.

Moonshot is also reportedly seeking $2 billion in funding at a valuation of around $30 billion. Its new model reflects how quickly Chinese AI companies are narrowing the performance gap with their American rivals while pursuing a more open approach to model distribution.
