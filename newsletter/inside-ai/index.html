Hello reader,

Infosys has picked its next CEO—Ashiss Kumar Dash, a 31-year company veteran who will take over from Salil Parekh in April 2027. An IIT-Kharagpur graduate, Dash has worked across software engineering, delivery, account management and sales, and helped establish Infosys’s development centre in Bhubaneswar. He currently leads the company’s services, utilities, resources, energy and sustainability business from Los Angeles. His appointment signals continuity: Dash will be expected to carry forward the AI strategy developed under Parekh, whose tenure saw Infosys grow from a $10 billion business into one generating more than $20 billion in annual revenue.

1️⃣Databricks and Microsoft deepen AI partnership

Photo by Shutterstock

Databricks and Microsoft have extended their decade-long partnership into the 2030s as they seek to help companies build AI tools around their own data and business knowledge. Databricks will use more of Microsoft’s Azure cloud platform to run its core operations and analytics, while adopting Microsoft’s Cobalt processors to improve computing efficiency. Microsoft, in turn, will integrate more Databricks products into its software. These include Genie, an AI assistant that allows employees to ask questions about company data using everyday language. The agreement strengthens Azure’s position as companies move AI projects from trials into regular operations.

2️⃣ Coforge wins $230 million AI transformation deal



Coforge has secured a five-year contract worth more than $230 million from an unnamed European company. The Indian IT services firm said it will lead an AI-driven overhaul of the client’s operations, although it has not disclosed further details about the customer or the work involved. The size and duration of the contract make it significant for Coforge and the wider Indian IT sector. It also suggests that companies are moving beyond small AI experiments and committing larger budgets to long-term implementation, creating a new source of business for technology service providers.

3️⃣ ServiceNow invests $40 million in BusinessNext



ServiceNow has invested $40 million in Noida-based BusinessNext, acquiring a stake of about 5 percent and valuing the banking software company at $700 million. BusinessNext provides AI-powered customer management tools to banks and financial institutions. The companies plan to combine its understanding of banking customers with ServiceNow’s software for managing internal processes. The aim is to help banks use customer information to make decisions and act on them more quickly across different departments. The partnership will also support BusinessNext’s expansion into markets, including Southeast Asia, Australia and New Zealand.

4️⃣ Alphabet’s AI spending tests its cash-rich model

Photo by Shutterstock

Alphabet reported negative quarterly free cash flow of $5.9 billion for the first time as spending on data centres and other AI infrastructure surged. The cash burn came despite Google Cloud revenue growing 82 percent, showing that demand for AI computing is rising alongside the cost of providing it. The company now expects to spend between $195 billion and $205 billion this year. In June, Alphabet announced a $84.75 billion share sale to finance its AI expansion. It had also raised more than $85 billion through debt over the preceding year, marking a notable shift towards outside funding.

5️⃣ Pronto turns service work into AI training data

Photo by REUTERS/Bhawika Chhabra

Indian home services startup Pronto is expanding Verified, an optional service that records professionals as they clean customers’ homes. Customers pay more for a Verified booking, while workers earn about twice the standard amount. The footage, captured through a downward-facing wearable camera without audio, is intended to provide customers and workers with a record of the job. Pronto says personal information is automatically blurred and the original recording deleted. Customers can access an anonymised version for 48 hours, after which the footage can be used to train AI systems to understand physical work.
