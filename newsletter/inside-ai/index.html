AI’s growing role in business is bringing questions about data and accountability into sharper focus. This week, Sarvam and developers backed by the IndiaAI Mission introduced models aimed at practical applications, from everyday business decisions to multilingual services. Alongside these releases, India’s latest proposals for sharing training data and auditing AI systems highlight the challenge of expanding access while protecting people and institutions. Together, these developments suggest that the terms on which AI is built and deployed are becoming as consequential as its capabilities.

Sarvam targets business decisions with Prism

Sarvam has launched Prism, an AI model for business decisions such as routing tasks and evaluating agents. Built on Sarvam 105B, it returns probabilities for predefined choices. Companies can customise its publicly available weights and deploy it themselves. Sarvam reports a median response time of 44 milliseconds in internal tests.

DEPA in AI vs consent debate

MeitY’s DEPA framework proposes enabling AI training on institutional datasets through controlled computing and privacy safeguards. While institutions retain data control, the handbook’s assertion that individual consent is impractical at scale raises questions about how personal information will be used and protected.

CERT-In standards for AI auditing

CERT-In’s new AI auditing guidelines classify systems into four risk tiers, linking scrutiny to potential harm. They cover transparency, bias, human oversight, six-hour reporting for qualifying incidents and 180-day log retention. Mint reports these guidelines are binding on empanelled auditors but remain non-binding for companies developing AI for now.

NPCI’s tech arm for AI & blockchain

The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has established NPCI Tech Solutions Limited, a wholly-owned subsidiary focussed on technology and innovation. Its remit includes artificial intelligence, blockchain, digital financial infrastructure and global capability centre services. The announcement adds a dedicated technology arm to the organisation behind India’s payments network.

IndiaAI grows homegrown portfolio

Three AI models developed with IndiaAI Mission support have been released: Soket Labs’ EKA-1 and ZenteiQ’s Dhīr 2B and 5B. EKA-1 is designed for coding and reasoning across English and 22 scheduled Indian languages. Dhīr targets multilingual services that can operate offline, including applications in government, defence, industry and education.

Enterprise customers drive OpenAI revenue

OpenAI now earns most of its revenue from enterprise customers, President Greg Brockman said. He believes the company will be ready for an IPO in 2027, although no listing timetable was confirmed. The company is expanding corporate sales, including through its Codex agent, as it competes with rival developer Anthropic.
