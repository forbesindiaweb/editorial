Diwali 2026 in Delhi-NCR: crackers past midnight, a lucky wind, and an official number with hours of data missing.

Diwali night, November 8. Firecrackers light up Delhi long after the Supreme Court’s 10pm cut-off, and the city’s air monitors start climbing towards their ceiling: 500.

Then, one by one, they fall silent. 39 stations went off the grid right after touching the maximum reading of 500.

The data blackout

Twelve hours, 247 missing

For the second Diwali in a row, Delhi’s air-quality data went missing during the peak pollution hours, between 10pm on Diwali night and 10am the next morning, just when firecracker smoke was at its worst.

Different averaging

India uses 24-hour averages for particulate matter; many apps use hourly or shorter windows and the US scale, with no upper cap. Neither number is “wrong”; they measure differently. Source: Central Pollution Control Board; US EPA; IQAir; AQI Live.

The rules

Two windows, all night

The Supreme Court allowed only certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR, and only for three hours a day, for two days before Diwali. The fireworks continued well past midnight.

Reading the air

How the number is made, and what it means for you

Every AQI figure in this story comes from a box on a pole. Here is how those stations work, why a few missing hours matter so much, and what each number means for the body.

01
Measures up to eight pollutantsPM2.5, PM10, nitrogen dioxide, ozone, carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide, ammonia and lead, depending on the station.
02
The worst pollutant sets the AQIEach pollutant gets its own sub-index. The highest one becomes the station’s AQI; on Diwali night, that is usually PM2.5.
03
Needs 16 hours a day to countA station’s 24-hour figure needs at least 16 hourly readings. Miss nine hours, and it drops out of the city’s average entirely.
04
Stops at 500India’s index ends at 500. A station can read “500” whether the air is just past the limit or far beyond it.
05
So timing is everythingWhen data vanishes in the worst hours, the cleaner hours carry more weight, and the city’s number looks better than the air was.
